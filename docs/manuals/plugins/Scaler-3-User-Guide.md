**Introduction > Installation & Registration 9** 

## Installation & Registration 

## **Downloading Scaler 3** 

Scaler 3 is available from either Scaler Music or Plugin Boutique. For details on redeeming your license and accessing the installer, refer to the instructions in your Scaler Music or Plugin Boutique account. 

Once you’ve downloaded the installer for macOS or Windows, double-click the file and follow the on-screen instructions. 

## **Installing Scaler 3** 

During installation, you can choose which plugin formats and components to install—such as VST2, VST3, AU (macOS only), AAX, and standalone—based on your system and preferences. 

## **Plugin Scanner** 

Scaler 3 can host third-party instrument and effect plugins directly on Chord Tracks and MIDI Lanes. 

As part of the installation process, Scaler 3’s Plugin Scanner will launch automatically. This step ensures that your audio plugins are properly located and available in Scaler 3. 

**==> picture [294 x 211] intentionally omitted <==**

## **Plugin Formats** 

By default, all plugin formats are selected for scanning to ensure Scaler 3 can locate your installed instruments. You can choose to include or exclude: VST, VST3, and AU (macOS only). 

## **Custom VST Paths** 

Scaler 3 allows you to specify up to three custom folders to be scanned for VST plugins, which is useful if your plugins are installed outside the default directories. 

- Add Paths – Click the **Add Custom VST Path** button to include additional folders. 

- Remove Paths – Use the × next to a folder to remove it from the scan.

## **Plugin Scan Controls** 

- Scan – Begins scanning using the selected formats and paths. 

- Skip – Bypasses the scan. You can scan plugins later by selecting 

   - “Rescan Your Plugins” from the Settings Menu > Preferences. 

_Note: Scanning may take some time depending on how many plugins are installed on your system._ 

Once the scan completes, the installer will finalise automatically. 

## **Activating Scaler 3** 

On first launch, Scaler 3 runs in trial mode for 14 days with full functionality. After the trial period expires, Scaler 3 must be activated to continue use. 

_Note: An internet connection is required during activation._ 

To activate your license: 

1. Open Scaler 3 in your DAW or launch the standalone version. 

2. Click the Scaler 3 logo in the top-left corner and choose Registration. 

3. Enter your license code and click **Activate Scaler 3** .

Once activated, Scaler 3 is fully licensed and ready to use. 

If you are connected to the internet when launching Scaler 3, the application will automatically check for updates. If a new version is available, a notification popup will appear with a link to your Scaler Music account page, where you can download and install the latest version. 

For troubleshooting tips or help with activation, visit scalermusic.com/support.

## Quick Start 

Browse Page Find a pre-made chord set using the browser in **Section A** . 

Find chords belonging to a particular scale using **Section B** .

Arrange Page Add tracks using the **+ icon** in the top left. This will display the **Motion browser** .

## DAW Sync 

The plugin version of Scaler 3 seamlessly integrates with your DAW, keeping everything in sync – including transport, tempo, time signature, and playback position. When DAW Sync is enabled, Scaler 3 follows your DAW’s transport controls, automatically updating its position to match your project timeline, including tempo, time signature, and bar position. 

- **Playback Sync** – Press play in your DAW, and Scaler 3 will follow along, staying locked to your arrangement. 

- **Dynamic Position Tracking** – Scaler 3 instantly jumps to the correct bar when you start playback and updates its position in real time based on your DAW’s transport, ensuring precise synchronization throughout your workflow. 

- **Loop Region** – When DAW Sync is enabled, Scaler 3’s loop region remains active and aligned with your DAW’s playhead. You can define this playback range using the Loop Markers, and it will stay fixed within Scaler – even if playback starts from different points in your DAW. Scaler will always begin from the corresponding position within the loop, making it easy to focus on specific sections of your timeline. 

- **Tempo & Time Signature Sync** – Scaler 3 automatically follows your DAW’s tempo and time signature, keeping all rhythmic elements aligned. 

**==> picture [23 x 20] intentionally omitted <==**

- With a loop region active on Scaler 3’s Arrange page, and DAW sync mode enabled, the bar at which Scaler will begin playback from is relative to your DAW’s playback position. For example, with a four bar loop active in Scaler 3, Scaler will treat every four bars of your DAW’s timeline as duplicates of its own four bar loop. So playing your DAW from bar 2 will start Scaler’s four bar loop from bar 2. Starting your DAW’s playback from bar 6 will also start Scaler at bar 2, as if Scaler’s loop region stretched from bars 5 - 8 in your DAW, instead of bars 1-4.

## Per Scale 

Per Scale mode suggests chords that belong to your current scale. This mode is useful for expanding progressions with suspended and extended chords while maintaining harmonic consistency. It analyzes movement between chords based on their degrees within the selected scale. 

For example, in the C Major scale, the relationship between G Major and C Major can be defined as V → I. Since both degrees exist in the selected scale, Scaler considers them harmonically linked. 

**==> picture [41 x 42] intentionally omitted <==**

**Browse Page > Suggest Mode 51** 

## Tonal 

Tonal mode suggests chords that complement your progression but may lie outside your current scale. It analyzes chord sets curated by musicians and artists exclusively for Scaler, allowing you to find harmonies that may not fit conventional scale-based music theory. This mode helps you explore unique voicings and add richness to your chord sequences. 

For example, the relationship between F Minor and C Major cannot be defined using the degrees of the C Major scale. However, F Minor is commonly played after C Major because of various harmonic connections: 

- Modal Interchange: F Minor exists in the C Phrygian, C Minor, and C Locrian modes. 

- Minor Subdominant: In harmonic analysis, F Minor functions as a minor subdominant (iv) of C Major. 

- Shared Scales: Both chords co-exist in C Harmonic Major, C Phrygian Dominant, and F Harmonic Minor. 

## **Auto-Detect** 

With Auto-Detect enabled, Scaler continuously analyzes your progression and updates the detected scale when you make changes. This ensures that chord suggestions remain relevant, especially when switching between Per Scale and Tonal modes. 

## **Minimize Movement** 

The Minimize Movement option helps keep suggested chords closer together in terms of voice leading. Enabling this option ensures smoother progressions by reducing large jumps between chords, making transitions feel more natural and connected. This is especially useful when aiming for cohesive voicings that maintain a consistent harmonic flow. 

## **Live Suggest Mode** 

Live Suggest Mode allows you to trigger suggested chords in real time when chord binding is enabled. Scaler 3 maps 8 suggested chords to MIDI keys starting from C4, letting you experiment with different harmonic options live as you play. 

This feature is useful for improvisation, real-time composition, or quickly testing harmonic possibilities with your MIDI controller. 

## **Pattern Fill** 

Clicking the Pattern Fill button generates a unique chord progression based on the selected suggestion mode (Tonal or Per Scale). This feature allows you to either extend your current pattern or create a fresh progression from scratch.

## Voice Grouping Profiles 

## **Dynamic** 

Allows for smooth changes between chords by automatically grouping notes around the tonal center of the currently selected scale. This prevents big jumps in intervals when moving from a lower diatonic chord to a higher diatonic chord, such as I > vi (for example, from a C Major to an A Minor chord). 

The Dynamic Grouping preset plays the bass note two octaves below the tonic, then automatically chooses the best inversions of each played chord to minimize their respective distance to the tonic of the scale. 

The upper extensions of the chord (9th, 11th, 13th) are grouped in a second octave unless the distance with the tonal center becomes too significant, in which case they are inverted and placed in the first octave. 

## **Dynamic C1-B2, C2-B3, C3-B4** 

Automatically adapts voicing to play chords within the defined range. Typically used for restricting the range of octaves when using sample libraries that only play across certain octaves.

**Appendix > Musical Notation 106** 

## Musical Notation 

Scaler uses a compact chord naming convention. The first part of the name is referring to the most general (non altered) part of the chord. 

For example, a min7 chord refers to a minor seventh chord made up of a minor third and minor seventh above the root. 

The second part are alterations applied to the chord. Alterations are optional and specified between parenthesis. Alterations affect the chord by taking precedence over the pitches that would normally establish the named chord. 

For example, a min7(b5) chord means that the expected perfect fifth of the minor seventh chord will be replaced by a diminished fifth, the rest of the pitches remain unchanged. 

The third part of the name is the extension. This part is also optional and is separated by spaces. 

For example, a min7(b5) b9 is a minor seventh chord with a diminished fifth to which we have added a flat nine. There can be multiple alterations and extensions to a chord. 

For example, a min11(b5 #9) is a minor eleventh chord with two alterations on the fifth and ninth, and a min7 b9 #11 is a minor seventh chord with no alterations but two extensions a flat ninth and a raised eleventh.
